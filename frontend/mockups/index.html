AlgoLearn: three design directions

Same product, same screens, same sample data (a 1487-rated student stuck on 1811/G2). Each direction differs in type, colour, density and motion. Open one, click through its screens, use its Theme button for light and dark, then pick one.

A · Module calm editorial

Feels like a well-made study module from a coaching institute, minus the clutter. Cool stone paper (not cream), a serif (Source Serif 4) for reading problems and hints, a plain sans (Public Sans) for controls, and one examiner-green ink. Low density with generous margins. Hatched meters plus numbers show weakness, never colour alone.

Motion: slow and settled. Things rise 10 px and fade in over 620 ms, buttons sink 1 px when pressed, the hero's paper sheets drift at different speeds, skeletons shimmer gently.

Why it fits: late-night upsolving needs calm and long reading comfort. The risk is that it can feel quiet, even a little too familiar.

B · Scorecard bold dark arena

A floodlit cricket scoreboard and broadcast graphics: night navy ground, chalk score boards, condensed uppercase type (Barlow Condensed), and exactly one yellow plate per screen for the thing that matters most. Dense: the dashboard is a scoreboard strip, a topic “wagon wheel”, a batting card of weak topics with last-five marks (■ □ ×), and the last 30 days as five overs, ball by ball. Dark first.

Motion: fast and clean. Graphics wipe in from the left (380 ms, 45 ms stagger), buttons snap to 96 % when pressed, the board and floodlight beams move on separate planes, skeletons sweep quickly.

Why it fits: Indian students read scorecards fluently, and topic strength reads like batting averages. The risk is energy: it has to stay a scorecard, never a game, and dense pages need care on phones.

C · Graph notebook playful but precise

The squared notebook every student practises in: graph-paper ground, ballpoint blue, a pink margin line, highlighter on what matters and sticky notes for hints and recommendations. Figtree for UI, Kalam (an Indian handwriting face) only for short margin notes. Medium density, with an index-tab sidebar instead of a top bar.

Motion: playful but exact. Notes land with a small spring and settle square, the highlighter swipes across key phrases, checkboxes bounce, floating notes drift above the page on scroll.

Why it fits: it feels like the student's own work, and highlighting the weak spot is the product's whole job. The risk is that handwriting overuse would turn childish, so it stays limited to notes.

True in all three

  • Every screen: landing, auth, dashboard (+ loading, empty, error), stuck input, stuck result (+ AI-unavailable fallback, loading, empty, error), problem detail with KaTeX, practice list, stuck history.
  • Press animation on buttons, staggered entrances, a parallax hero and skeleton shimmer, all switched off by prefers-reduced-motion.
  • Hints reveal one at a time, and the full solution sits behind a deliberate second button. AI output is labelled as AI, and practice-only copy appears on the landing page, auth and stuck input.
  • Sample data with real Codeforces problems and tags. No testimonials, user counts or Codeforces branding.
